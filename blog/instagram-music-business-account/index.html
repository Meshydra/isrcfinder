Why business accounts see less music on Instagram

16 November 2026 · 2 min read

If your Instagram music picker shows unfamiliar instrumental tracks and none of the songs everyone else is using, your account is probably set up as a business account. It is a licensing matter, not a fault.

Why the library is different

The deals Meta has with record labels and publishers cover personal, non-commercial use: someone sharing a moment with friends. They generally do not cover a company using a hit song to promote a product. That kind of use normally needs its own licence, agreed directly with the rights holders.

So Instagram gives business accounts a different catalogue: royalty-free music that is cleared for commercial use. Meta publishes it as the Meta Sound Collection. It has thousands of tracks and sound effects, but not the charts.

How to tell this is your situation

  • Almost every well-known song is missing, not just one.
  • The tracks you do see are mostly instrumental or by names you do not recognise.
  • A friend with a personal account in the same country sees the songs you cannot.

If only one or two songs are missing, the cause is more likely country licensing or search. See why a song is not available on Instagram.

Your options, within the rules

Use the royalty-free catalogue

This is the safe choice for a business. The tracks are cleared for commercial posts, so there is no risk of a video being muted later.

License the song properly

If a specific song matters to a campaign, the right route is a licence from the rights holders. For larger brands that is a routine cost. For small businesses it is usually more than the post is worth.

Use original audio

Your own voiceover, sounds you recorded, or music you commissioned and own are always yours to use.

About switching account type

Instagram lets you change between account types in settings, and personal and creator accounts see the wider library. Keep in mind what the library is for. If the account promotes a business, using licensed chart music for that promotion can still fall outside what the music licences allow, whichever account type is selected. Posts can be muted, and rights holders can raise claims. Read Meta's music guidelines before relying on it.

Where an ISRC helps

An ISRC search does not widen your library. What it does is remove the guesswork when a song should be available to you and title search is not finding it. Look the song up in MusicFinder, copy the code, and search Instagram with it. If the exact recording is in your library, that is the result you get. If nothing comes back, the track is not offered to your account.

Summary

Business accounts get a smaller library because licensed music is for personal use. The choices that will not cause trouble later are the royalty-free catalogue, a proper licence, or audio you own.
